\begin{abstract}
The evolution toward 6G is driven by stringent latency, reliability, and
throughput requirements of Digital-Twin (DT) applications such as smart
warehouses, autonomous systems, and intelligent environments. In these
settings, the wireless network is no longer a passive medium but an active
component that must adapt continuously to user position, mobility, and
environmental geometry. This motivates situation-aware operation, where the
antenna system anticipates rather than reacts to channel conditions.
This paper presents a situation-aware Integrated Sensing and Communication
(ISAC) framework that couples NVIDIA Sionna RT for GPU-accelerated, site-specific
ray tracing with ns-3/5G~LENA for full-stack NR networking. The framework is
organised into four modules: a radar-inspired sensing module that converts
ray-traced multipath into Equivalent Reflection Points (ERPs) and applies
$Z$-gating, Moving-Target Indication (MTI), and DBSCAN clustering;
a multi-target tracking module that combines a constant-velocity Kalman filter
with Hungarian data association; a sensing-informed adaptive beamforming module
that synthesises a multi-lobe Gaussian radiation pattern toward confirmed
tracks; and a communication module that exports per-path channel state to ns-3.
The framework is validated in two stages. A controlled stage exercises the
pipeline in free-space, warehouse, and urban-micro street-canyon scenes with
six ablation variants. An application-oriented stage embeds the framework in
an end-to-end smart-warehouse Digital Twin with mixed mobile-robot, sensor, and
video traffic, evaluated for $2{\times}2$, $4{\times}4$, and $8{\times}8$ gNB
arrays. Results show up to $18$~dB reduction in mean path loss in free space,
detection rates above $0.84$, and---in the warehouse use case---a $4{\times}4$
array as the balanced operating point, with roughly $25$~dB reduction in
robot-link path loss and improved aggregated throughput at near-baseline energy
cost. The proposed framework is therefore viable for deployment-oriented ISAC
studies once sufficient gNB array support is available.
\end{abstract}
