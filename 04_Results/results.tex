\section{Experimental Evaluation}
\label{sec:results}

This section evaluates the proposed framework. Section~\ref{subsec:eval_method}
defines the scope and methodology. Sections~\ref{subsec:exp1_setup}
and~\ref{subsec:exp2_setup} describe the two experiments---a controlled
scene-level study and the warehouse Digital-Twin study. Sections~\ref{subsec:exp1_results}
and~\ref{subsec:exp2_results} present the corresponding results.
Section~\ref{subsec:discussion} discusses cross-experiment implications.

\subsection{Evaluation Scope and Methodology}
\label{subsec:eval_method}

The evaluation is organised in two complementary stages with a shared
measurement logic. The first (Experiment~I) is a controlled validation that
operates at the SionnaRT level under deterministic trajectories, fixed radio
parameters, and three canonical scenes. It isolates the implemented sensing
and beamforming logic from upper-layer effects so that scene-driven changes
in communication and sensing outputs can be observed cleanly. The second
(Experiment~II) couples the same framework to a full NR stack and to the
warehouse Digital-Twin workload of Section~\ref{subsec:warehouse}, examining
whether the framework remains beneficial under mixed traffic, autonomous
mobility, and end-to-end protocol behaviour.

In both stages a baseline (situation-awareness disabled) is compared against
ISAC-enabled configurations using a common metric grouping:
\emph{communication quality} (path loss, delay, throughput, jitter, packet
loss); \emph{sensing quality} (detection rate, false-positive rate,
missed-detection rate, mean position error, track continuity, mean track run
length); and \emph{system cost} (consumed energy, average power, energy
efficiency).

\subsection{Experiment I: Controlled Validation Setup}
\label{subsec:exp1_setup}

Three scenes are used: a free-space scene with the transmitter at
$[0,-10,10]$~m, a warehouse scene with the same transmitter position, and an
urban-micro street-canyon scene with the transmitter at $[0,-20,8]$~m
(Fig.~\ref{fig:exp1_scenes}). The free-space and warehouse scenes use the
\texttt{iw\_hub} mobile-robot mesh as the target object; the street-canyon
scene uses a car mesh. Three targets follow deterministic planar
trajectories---sinusoidal in the indoor scenes, lane-aligned in the
street-canyon---over $100$ time steps at $0.2$~s resolution, for a total
observation window of $20$~s.

Seven configurations are compared. \texttt{baseline} disables situation

beamwidth $W=18^\circ$ and propagation depth $3$. \texttt{isac\_no\_mti}

to~$5$; \texttt{isac\_no\_tracker} disables the tracker. At each step,
estimated track positions are matched to ground truth with the Hungarian
algorithm; unmatched ground-truth targets count as missed detections,
unmatched estimates as false positives, and matched-pair distances as
localisation errors.

\subsection{Experiment I: Controlled-Validation Results}
\label{subsec:exp1_results}

\subsubsection{Free-space scene}
The free-space results are summarised in Tables~\ref{tab:exp1_fs_prop}
and~\ref{tab:exp1_fs_sense}. Relative to the baseline, all ISAC variants
reduce mean path loss by $15.6$--$18.0$~dB, with corresponding gains in
received power, while mean delay remains constant at $54.10$~ns---signal
strength dominates the change. Among the ablations, \texttt{isac\_wide}
gives the lowest mean path loss ($84.39$~dB).

\subsubsection{Warehouse scene}
Tables~\ref{tab:exp1_wh_prop} and~\ref{tab:exp1_wh_sense} report the
warehouse-scene results. Path loss reductions are smaller than in free space
($7.4$--$14.7$~dB) but still consistent; \texttt{isac\_wide} again yields the
strongest communication. Sensing, however, degrades sharply: detection rate

$1.0$--$1.13$~m for the tracked variants, so localisation is reasonably
accurate \emph{when} an object is detected, but the dominant limitation is
the frequency of missed detections. \texttt{isac\_deep} and
\texttt{isac\_no\_tracker} produce substantially higher false-positive

\subsubsection{Cross-scene summary}
Free space provides the strongest joint sensing-and-communication performance
and confirms the framework's expected upper bound. The warehouse scene
preserves communication gains but exposes a strong sensing penalty from
indoor clutter. The street canyon shows that, once a confined geometry
dominates propagation, communication becomes nearly invariant to ISAC
ablations while sensing remains configuration-sensitive. Across all three
scenes, the tracked variants consistently produce cleaner outputs than
\texttt{isac\_no\_tracker}, confirming that temporal association is required
for stable ISAC behaviour.

\subsection{Experiment II: Warehouse Use-Case Results}
\label{subsec:exp2_results}

\subsubsection{Application-level KPIs}
The application-layer comparison (Fig.~\ref{fig:exp2_app}) shows that the
main differences between baseline and ISAC are concentrated on the
controller, package-sensor, and robot traffic. At $2\!\times\!2$, controller
throughput drops from $417.674$ to $205.345$~kbps under ISAC, and
package-sensor throughput from $22.990$ to $6.634$~kbps; robot throughput
slightly increases from $1159.444$ to $1249.326$~kbps but is accompanied by
$15.439\%$ packet loss, so the $2\!\times\!2$ ISAC configuration is
characterised by unstable delivery. At $4\!\times\!4$, the controller and
package-sensor throughputs return to baseline levels and robot throughput
rises to $1582.081$~kbps versus a baseline of $1328.452$~kbps. The same
robot-dominant gain persists at $8\!\times\!8$ ($1445.378$~kbps), without
proportional additional benefit. The principal communication gain of ISAC
in the warehouse therefore appears once the gNB array reaches
$4\!\times\!4$.

\subsubsection{Propagation KPIs}
Fig.~\ref{fig:exp2_prop} explains the throughput trends. Robot path loss in
the baseline drops from $83.948$~dB at $2\!\times\!2$ to roughly $69.2$~dB at
$4\!\times\!4$ and $8\!\times\!8$; under ISAC the same trend is stronger,
falling from $82.318$~dB at $2\!\times\!2$ to $57.359$~dB at $4\!\times\!4$
and remaining essentially unchanged at $8\!\times\!8$. The rack-sensor links
also benefit, with about a $20$~dB reduction relative to the baseline at all
three array sizes. By contrast, the package, temperature, and humidity
sensors all see higher path loss under ISAC. The propagation improvement is
therefore selective: it is concentrated on the links that are most relevant
for mobile warehouse operation (the robots) and this selective gain drives
the system-level throughput improvement at $4\!\times\!4$ and beyond.

\subsubsection{Energy KPIs}
The principal scaling factor for power consumption is the gNB array size:
gNB average power rises from $\approx 25.6$~W at $2\!\times\!2$ to
$\approx 51.3$~W at $4\!\times\!4$ and $\approx 145$~W at $8\!\times\!8$,
with corresponding gNB energy of $\approx 3.1$, $\approx 6.15$, and
$\approx 17.4$~kJ. UE-side power is approximately invariant across array
sizes because all UEs share a $2\!\times\!2$ antenna configuration. At the
system level, total reported energy is nearly identical between baseline and
ISAC for the $4\!\times\!4$ and $8\!\times\!8$ cases while ISAC achieves
slightly higher throughput, so the $4\!\times\!4$ ISAC configuration improves
end-to-end performance without significant additional system-level energy
cost.

\subsubsection{Sensing and Localisation}
Fig.~\ref{fig:exp2_sense} reports the warehouse sensing KPIs derived from
active robot opportunities and validated detected-object records. Detection
rate rises from $0.485$ at $2\!\times\!2$ to $0.523$ at $4\!\times\!4$, with

correspondingly falls from $0.515$ to $\approx 0.477$. Validated false
positives are already low at $2\!\times\!2$ ($0.021$) and drop to zero for
$4\!\times\!4$ and $8\!\times\!8$. Track continuity remains close to unity
across all three configurations, indicating that once a valid detection
sequence is formed the exported track identities are stable.

The localisation-error distribution (Fig.~\ref{fig:exp2_loc}) shows that

\emph{stability}---suppressing large outliers and sustaining more valid
matched opportunities---rather than pointwise median precision.

\subsection{Discussion}
\label{subsec:discussion}

The two experiments show that the proposed ISAC framework behaves
consistently but in a strongly condition-dependent manner. Under controlled
conditions, the framework responds to scene geometry and ablation choices in
a physically meaningful way: free space yields the most favourable regime
with strong communication gains and stable sensing when the tracker is
retained; the warehouse scene preserves communication gains but introduces a
substantial sensing penalty in completeness and continuity; and the urban
micro scene shows that strongly geometry-driven propagation can decouple
communication stability from sensing stability.

The warehouse Digital-Twin study extends these observations into a
deployment-relevant context. Practical applicability depends not only on
whether ISAC is enabled but also on whether the radio configuration provides
sufficient operating margin. The $2\!\times\!2$ ISAC configuration fails to
sustain competitive end-to-end communication, while the $4\!\times\!4$ and
$8\!\times\!8$ configurations restore system-level competitiveness and
slightly exceed the baseline in throughput and efficiency. The
$8\!\times\!8$ configuration does not materially improve upon $4\!\times\!4$
in either communication or sensing while doubling and tripling the gNB power
draw, identifying $4\!\times\!4$ as the balanced operating point for the
tested workload.

The sensing analysis reinforces this conclusion: larger gNB arrays do not
primarily reduce median localisation error, but they suppress large-error
outliers and sustain a larger pool of valid matched opportunities. In
deployment terms, this is the form of stability that is most useful, because
isolated low-error detections accompanied by frequent misses or unstable
tracks are of limited practical value.

At the same time, the results reveal residual constraints. Sensing is
significantly more fragile than communication in cluttered environments;
communication gains are not uniform across device classes, so application
-level benefits remain workload-dependent; and the warehouse sensing
evaluation is currently derived from exported detections and reconstructed
opportunity definitions rather than from a complete tracker-side confusion
matrix. These observations frame the future-work agenda discussed in
Section~\ref{sec:conclusion}.
