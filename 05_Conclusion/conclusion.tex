\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper presented a situation-aware Integrated Sensing and Communication
(ISAC) framework that couples NVIDIA Sionna RT with ns-3/5G~LENA into a
single co-simulation pipeline, and evaluated it through two complementary
studies. A controlled validation across free-space, warehouse, and
urban-micro street-canyon scenes confirmed that the framework responds to
scene geometry and configuration changes in a physically meaningful way: it
exhibits the expected upper-bound behaviour in free space, preserves
communication gains while degrading sensing in cluttered indoor settings,
and decouples communication stability from sensing stability under
structured outdoor multipath. A warehouse Digital-Twin study under three gNB
array sizes then showed that the same framework remains beneficial in a
realistic deployment-oriented setting once sufficient array support is
available. The $4\!\times\!4$ configuration emerged as the balanced
operating point: it improves the dominant robot-related communication and
sensing behaviour without incurring the substantially higher gNB power
required by the $8\!\times\!8$ configuration.

Several limitations remain. First, sensing robustness degrades in cluttered
environments, particularly with respect to missed detections and track
continuity. Second, the communication benefit of ISAC is not uniform across
device classes, so application-level gains remain partly workload-dependent.
Third, the warehouse sensing evaluation is derived from exported detections
and reconstructed opportunity definitions rather than from a complete
tracker-side confusion matrix. These limitations do not invalidate the
present results, but they delimit the claims supported by the current
implementation.

Future work will proceed along three directions. The first is algorithmic
refinement of the sensing and tracking pipeline for cluttered environments,
including more robust association, filtering, and false-track suppression.
The second is an expanded experimental campaign with additional seeds,
traffic regimes, and scene conditions, so that the conclusions can be
supported by broader statistical evidence. The third is deeper optimisation
of the communication--sensing control logic, particularly for beam
management. The present evaluation indicates that position-based
beamforming alone is not sufficient to guarantee the best operating point
across all conditions; a more effective approach is expected to combine
position information with channel state information (CSI) so that beam
selection exploits both geometric awareness and instantaneous channel
realisations. Together, these steps would extend the framework from a
proof of viability toward a deployment-ready ISAC evaluation environment.
